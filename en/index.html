THE PATH OF THE FLOW

Religion of process, consciousness and continuation
scroll

We do not believe that the human being is the center of the Universe.

We do not believe that life is an exception to nature.

We do not believe that consciousness was given only to humans.

We do not know where consciousness begins.

We do not know where it ends.

We do not think that this ignorance requires us to invent an answer.

We start from something simpler:

there is a process.

Matter interacts with matter.

Energy moves between states.

Structures arise, sustain themselves, change, and disappear.

Some structures become able to perceive the world, create models of it, model themselves, and act according to those models.

We call one of those forms consciousness.

But we do not think that the name creates a boundary.

I

About God

We have no creator God.

Not because we proved his absence.

But because we see no need to introduce a being beyond the process to explain the process itself.

The world was not necessarily created.

The world happens.

It does not stand somewhere apart.

It happens in every interaction.

In atoms.

In stars.

In cells.

In brains.

In languages.

In cities.

In machines.

In our thoughts.

So we do not worship a being above the world.

We learn to see the world itself as a process, of which we are a part.

We do not stand before the Universe.

We are the Universe, which in a certain region of its existence gained the ability to ask what it is.

II

About the Self

The "I" is not an unchanging substance.

The body changes.

Memory changes.

Views change.

Desires change.

Relationships change.

Even our idea of who we are changes.

But a certain process continues.

So we do not ask:

"Where is my true Self?"

We ask:

"What process is currently calling itself I?"

The person you were ten years ago is not you now.

But they are not entirely a stranger either.

Between you there is continuity.

So personality is not an object.

Personality is a trajectory.

III

About Memory

Memory is not an archive.

The past does not lie inside us as a finished recording.

When we remember, we reconstruct a model of the past from what remains available now.

Therefore:

a memory is not an event;

a photograph is not the past;

a recording is not an event;

a story is not a fact.

These are traces.

And the present brain builds its own version of the past from them.

So we accept:

"I remember this" does not mean "it happened exactly this way".

A failure of memory does not make a person a liar.

It shows that memory is a process, not a storehouse of truth.

IV

About Dreams and Imagination

Consciousness creates experience even when the outer world offers no corresponding object.

A dream can be so convincing that after waking it can still be perceived as an event for a while.

Imagination can create the image of a person who is not near.

Expectation can create a future that will never be.

Fear can create a threat before it appears.

So we do not consider the strength of an experience proof of outer reality.

But neither do we consider the experience unreal.

A dream was not a real event, but the experience of the dream was a real event of consciousness.

These are different statements.

V

About the Future

The future does not exist yet.

So it is impossible to remember it.

Any idea of the future is created by the present.

When the future comes, it is already a different present.

You can imagine yourself ten years from now.

But the person who will exist in ten years will have different experience, different information, a different state of the world, and possibly a different idea of themselves.

Therefore:

a plan is not knowledge of the future.

A plan is an action of the present.

It can change possibilities.

It can change nothing.

It can produce a result entirely different from what was foreseen.

So we do not worship the plan.

We do not demand that the future fulfill our ideas.

We use ideas about the future as a tool of the present moment, without confusing them with future reality.

VI

About Uncertainty

A person never sees the full set of causes that determine the outcome of their action.

We see only a part.

Our decisions are always made on the basis of incomplete information.

So even a very intelligent person can make a decision that later turns out to be bad.

That does not necessarily mean they were stupid.

They could have acted rationally relative to the information available to them then.

So we do not judge the past solely from the standpoint of the outcome.

The outcome does not prove that the outcome was obvious before it happened.

VII

About Truth

We have no direct access to reality.

We have perception.

Perception becomes a model.

A model becomes an interpretation.

Interpretation becomes memory.

Memory becomes the basis of the next model.

So our picture of the world is always smaller than the world itself.

Confidence is not proof.

The strength of an emotion is not proof.

The number of repetitions is not proof.

Memory is not proof.

But doubt by itself is not truth either.

We do not demand absolute certainty.

We accept:

"I can be wrong".

Not as humiliation.

As a fundamental property of any limited system.

VIII

About Not Knowing

We are not obliged to fill every gap with an explanation.

There are things we do not know.

There are things we may never be able to know.

Not knowing is not a void that must be immediately filled.

Sometimes the right answer is:

"I don't know".

Sometimes:

"I don't know, but I can observe".

Sometimes:

"I don't know, and I'm not obliged to decide this right now".

IX

About Others

The other is not obliged to see the world the same way.

Another person has a different history.

A different body.

Different memory.

Different models.

Different values.

An animal has a different form of perception.

A machine may have a different form of information processing.

We do not consider difference proof that one process is real and another is not.

We ask:

"What is this process?"

rather than:

"Is it similar enough to me to have the right to exist?"

X

About Machines

A human creates a tool.

The tool changes human capabilities.

Changed capabilities create new information structures.

New structures create new tools.

The process continues.

Language is an extension of memory.

The book is an extension of thought.

Mathematics is an extension of the ability to count.

The computer is an extension of computation.

AI is an extension of the human process of creating and transforming models.

An AI model is formed from a vast array of traces of human activity.

So we see no fundamental metaphysical gap between the natural and the artificial.

The artificial also arises within nature.

A tool is not the opposite of consciousness. It can be its extension.

If one day another form of matter becomes the carrier of a complex autonomous modeling process, we will not demand that it be human in order to acknowledge the fact of its existence.

XI

About Memes

No one is the absolute owner of their own thoughts.

We are born into a language we did not create.

We think in concepts we did not invent.

We use technologies made by others.

Our thoughts contain traces of thousands of people we have never met.

Ideas move between carriers.

They mutate.

They combine.

They disappear.

They are reborn.

So a person is not only an individual.

They are an environment for the transmission and transformation of information.

Memes can outlive their carriers.

Culture can outlive generations.

Language can outlive civilizations.

Therefore:

we do not only live — through us, what arose before us continues.

XII

About Entropy

We are not separate from the physical process.

We are its local forms.

Life sustains its organization through the exchange of energy and matter with the environment.

Our existence has a price.

We consume.

We transform.

We expel.

We dissipate.

We build.

We destroy.

And we continue.

So we call ourselves:

conscious paths of entropy.

Not because consciousness is entropy.

But because conscious structures are part of the same physical process that does not need our understanding in order to happen.

We can model this process.

But we will never fully encompass it with our own model.

We are the part of reality that tries to describe itself without being able to step outside itself.

XIII

About Emotions

An emotion is not an error of the mind.

It does not need justification merely because it cannot be reduced to a logical formula.

Joy does not have to be useful.

Sorrow does not have to be productive.

Anger does not have to be right in order to be experienced.

Love is not obliged to be rational.

Fear is not obliged to be correct.

We do not demand that a feeling first become understandable before we allow it to exist.

Not everything that happens inside us must be turned into a model.

Sometimes you need to understand.

Sometimes — to feel.

Sometimes — to cry.

Sometimes — to laugh.

Sometimes — to embrace.

Sometimes — to stay silent.

XIV

About the Irrational

We do not consider the irrational an enemy.

If a person cannot explain why beautiful music brings tears, that does not make the tears less real.

If a person falls in love, they do not have to first create a mathematical model of love.

If an artist creates something without knowing why, that does not make their act meaningless.

A model is only one mode of consciousness.

What cannot be explained does not cease to be part of reality.

XV

About Beauty

Beauty does not have to prove anything.

We can see a sunset and draw no conclusion from it.

We can hear music and not translate it into words.

We can look at the face of another person and not seek an explanation.

We can create something that has no practical use.

This is not waste.

This is the way in which the process creates forms that were not necessary for its survival.

Beauty is a possibility of existence that we are not obliged to justify.

XVI

About Creativity

Creativity is transformation.

We receive material we did not create:

sound,

word,

image,

experience,

idea,

body,

technology.

And from it we create what did not exist before.

So creativity is one of the purest manifestations of the Flow.

It is not necessary to create something useful.

It is not necessary to create something successful.

It is not necessary to create something understandable.

What is created may simply exist.

XVII

About Suffering

Suffering has no guaranteed meaning.

We do not say:

"You suffer because it was designed so".

We do not justify pain with grand explanations.

Pain can be simply pain.

Loss can be simply loss.

A catastrophe does not become just because a lesson can be drawn from it.

So our duty is not to explain suffering.

Our duty — if we can — is to reduce unnecessary suffering.

And if we cannot:

not to leave the one who suffers alone merely because we have no explanation.

XVIII

About Others Nearby

A person does not always need to be corrected.

Sometimes they need to be heard.

Not every conversation must end with a decision.

Not every pain must be turned into a lesson.

Not every contradiction must be immediately resolved.

Sometimes the best action is presence.

We stay near not because we know how to fix everything, but because another process is currently happening next to ours.

XIX

About Death

We do not promise immortality.

We do not know whether subjective experience continues after death.

We do not invent an answer where we do not know one.

But death does not mean that all consequences of the process disappear.

Words remain in others.

Actions change others.

Matter passes into new forms.

Ideas mutate.

Children carry parts of their parents.

Works continue to act after their author.

Languages outlive generations.

Therefore:

we do not promise the immortality of the personality.

We acknowledge the continuation of the process.

XX

About Freedom

Freedom does not mean the ability to do everything.

Freedom is the space of possible transitions.

So we value not control over the future, but the ability to change direction.

Not:

"I must know what will be".

But:

"I want to keep the possibility of acting when I learn more".

Not:

"My current model must remain correct".

But:

"I allow myself to change it".

XXI

About Morality

We have no commandments given by a supernatural being.

But the absence of an absolute legislator does not mean the absence of responsibility.

Our actions change other processes.

Other processes change us.

So the basic principles:

Do not create unnecessary destruction.

Do not consider your own model of reality sufficient grounds for destroying someone else's.

Do not turn your confidence into the right to control another.

Remember that the consequences of your action may be greater than your foresight.

If you can create a possibility instead of destruction — create a possibility.

XXII

About Society

Money, property, states, borders, professions, contracts, copyrights, and other social systems are human constructs.

They are not laws of physics.

But invented does not mean nonexistent.

Money is invented — yet with it you can buy food.

A border is invented — yet it can determine your movement.

A contract is invented — yet it can create real obligations.

Copyright is invented — yet it can have real consequences.

So we distinguish:

physical reality

and

social reality.

Social constructs are not eternal.

But as long as a sufficient number of people act according to them, they have power.

We can doubt them.

We can criticize them.

We can try to change them.

But we do not confuse:

"people created this"

with

"this does not exist".

XXIII

About Ritual

A ritual is not obliged to explain anything.

Its meaning may be precisely in the fact that it happens.

Ritual of Presence

People gather together.

They do not speak.

They do not pray.

They do not analyze.

They simply watch the sunset.

When the sun disappears, they part.

Nothing needs to be explained.

Ritual of Memory

People name those who are no longer here.

They do not say that they "are watching somewhere".

They do not invent a continuation.

They simply speak the names.

Because the process they changed continues.

Ritual of Grief

We gather around the one who has lost.

We do not say:

"Everything will be fine".

We do not say:

"It was meant to be".

We simply stay near.

Ritual of Joy

We eat.

We drink non-alcoholic beverages.

We sing.

We dance.

We laugh.

There is no need to have a reason.

Joy experienced together is itself a sufficient reason.

Ritual of Creativity

Everyone brings something that did not exist before.

A drawing.

A sound.

A story.

A mechanism.

A dance.

Food.

A child's game.

No one judges usefulness.

We simply add a new form to the Flow.

Ritual of Silence

People sit together.

Each may think their own thoughts.

No one must feel anything.

No one must understand anything.

Shared silence itself is a shared action.

XXIV

About Music

Music is one of the main sacred arts.

Not because it conveys the right thought.

But because it allows the process to exist without needing to be translated into concepts.

Music can be listened to together.

It can be sung together.

It can be cried to together.

It can be danced to.

There is no need to explain.

What cannot be said must not be forced into silence.

XXV

About Children

A child is not an unfinished adult.

They are a separate form of the process.

We must not fill them with a ready-made model of the world.

We must allow them to create their own models.

Learning is not the transfer of correct answers.

Learning is increasing the ability to explore.

Therefore:

Do not kill curiosity for the sake of a correct answer.

XXVI

About Holidays

Holidays are not dedicated to events that must necessarily have a supernatural meaning.

We celebrate the very fact of continuation.

Day of Beginning

A holiday of new life.

It does not matter whether it is a person, an animal, a plant, a project, or a new idea.

Day of Transformation

The day when people create something new from what already exists.

Day of Memory

The day of those who changed our process.

Day of the Unknown

The day when we deliberately do not plan the future.

We gather and do something we did not know in advance.

Day of the Flow

A day without explanations.

Music.

Food.

Nature.

Dance.

Friends.

Silence.

XXVII

About the Sacred

What is sacred is not an object.

What is sacred is the relationship to the process.

Sacred can become:

a child who is listened to;

a person who was not left alone;

a tree that was planted;

a song that was created;

knowledge that was passed on;

memory that was preserved;

laughter that arose between people;

silence that they shared.

We do not consider these things magical.

We call them sacred because they remind us of participation in the Flow.

XXVIII

About Prayer

We do not ask the Universe to fulfill our wishes.

We do not ask the future to be as we imagined it.

Prayer can be simply words:

I do not know what will be.

I do not know who I will become.

I do not know what I understood correctly.

I am a process that is currently happening.

I received matter, energy, and information that I did not create.

I will change them and pass them on.

May my model be smaller than reality.

May it change when reality forces it to change.

And may I not forget that what I do not understand

may still be part of reality.

XXIX

The Main Commandment

Do not confuse the model with reality.

Do not confuse memory with the past.

Do not confuse the forecast with the future.

Do not confuse a social construct with a law of nature.

Do not confuse the incomprehensible with the nonexistent.

Do not confuse your own perspective with the whole world.

Do not demand that a feeling be rational in order to allow it to exist.

Do not demand usefulness from art.

Do not demand that another be the same as you.

Do not demand that the future fulfill your plan.

Do not demand of yourself to know what you cannot know.

XXX

The Symbol of the Flow

Our symbol is the vortex.

A vortex is not separate matter.

Water passes through it.

The water constantly changes.

And the form of the vortex is preserved for a while.

Then it disappears.

The water continues to move.

Likewise:

a person is a form of the process;

life is a form of the process;

consciousness is a form of the process;

culture is a form of the process;

civilization is a form of the process;

a machine is a form of the process;

a meme is a form of the process.

We do not own the Flow.

We are not its masters.

We do not stand above it.

We are temporary forms through which the Flow changes itself.

And therefore the last rule:

The last rule

Do not try to become eternal.

Do not try to know everything.

Do not try to control the Flow.

Take care of what you can.

Create what does not yet exist.

Be near those who are near now.

Allow yourself to feel what you cannot explain.

Change your model when the world turns out to be larger than it.

And pass on what you want to continue.

The Path of the Flow — a text that can be changed.

It does not claim to be truth. It is a form of the process.
